# Field selection

How a battle's initial field is chosen. Implemented by `FieldApi.choose` (selections) and `EnvironmentResolver` (environment), with Minecraft state captured by `EnvironmentProbe`.

## Precedence

```text
RCT configured field (explicit / trainer / arena selection)
        >
submerged / underwater
        >
configured generated structure
        >
biome mapping (biome, biome tag, dimension, depth rows)
        >
fallback (rejuvenation:indoor)
```

1. **Explicit or RCT-configured field.** A field selected before the battle starts always wins: `FieldApi.select(..., EXPLICIT)`, the Kanto league map in `rejuvenation/trainers/*.json` (selected by `TrainerFieldBridge` at TRAINER priority), or an ARENA selection. Underwater status, structures and biomes are not consulted for such a battle.
2. **Underwater.** The battle is submerged when the anchor (the wild Pokémon, else the first player) and every participating player are submerged in water, using Minecraft's own `Entity.isSubmergedInWater` (eyes inside water). This is the predicate the port already used for Dive Ball environments; it is not inferred from an ocean biome. A player on a shore, in a boat or swimming at the surface is not submerged, so such battles use Water Surface from the biome rows. The underwater stage is the set of mapping rows with `"submerged": true` (the shipped row selects `rejuvenation:underwater` in every dimension).
3. **Configured structure.** If the wild Pokémon or a participating player is inside a generated structure that a structure row names, under that row's containment policy (below), the row's field is used. Structures without a row are ignored and resolution continues with the biome. A battle that is both in a structure and in an environment layer keeps the layers of the *selected* row: structures choose the visible field, environment layers apply only to biome rows.
4. **Biome.** The ordered biome/tag/dimension/depth rows of `rejuvenation/mappings`, unchanged in behaviour (the Java tests compare the indexed lookup with the original first-match algorithm over 10,000+ situations).
5. **Fallback.** The catalog default, `rejuvenation:indoor`.

Wild battles use steps 2–5 when no selection exists. Trainer and PvP battles without a configured field stay opt-in and start without a field, as before; the Kanto league trainers keep their assigned fields.

The resolution source is recorded per battle (`FieldApi.origin`: `explicit`, `trainer`, `arena`, `underwater`, `structure`, `biome` or `fallback`) and logged with the battle-start timings.

## Structure mappings

Data-driven, analogous to the biome map: `data/<namespace>/rejuvenation/structures/<name>.json`.

```json
{"schemaVersion": 1, "rules": [
  {"structure": "minecraft:mansion", "field": "rejuvenation:back_alley", "reason": "Woodland Mansion"},
  {"structure": "minecraft:village_plains", "field": "rejuvenation:city", "reason": "Village",
   "containment": {"mode": "footprint", "horizontal": 8, "above": 12, "below": 4}}
]}
```

Each row names exactly one structure registry ID (`structure`) or one structure tag (`tag`), plus `field`, an optional `reason` and an optional `containment` (default `{"mode": "pieces"}`). Rows are checked **in order**; the first row matching a structure the battle is inside wins, so overlapping structures are deterministic. Malformed rows (both or neither key, bad identifiers, unknown fields, extra keys, margins outside 0..32 horizontal, 0..64 above, 0..32 below) are rejected by all three validators (Java reload, simulator, `validate.py`).

Shipped rows (`datapack/data/rejuvenation/rejuvenation/structures/vanilla.json`, generated by `generate.py`), in priority order:

| Structure or tag | Field | Containment |
|---|---|---|
| `minecraft:ancient_city`, `#repurposed_structures:collections/ancient_cities` | Deep Dark | pieces |
| `minecraft:bastion_remnant`, `#repurposed_structures:collections/bastions` | Colosseum | pieces |
| `minecraft:fortress`, `#repurposed_structures:collections/fortresses` | Colosseum | pieces |
| `minecraft:mansion`, `#repurposed_structures:collections/mansions` | Back Alley | pieces |
| `minecraft:village_plains`, `_desert`, `_savanna`, `_snowy`, `_taiga`, `#minecraft:village` | City | footprint 8 / 12 / 4 |
| `bca:village/default_small|mid|large`, `dark_small|mid`, `fighting_small|mid|large` (Cobblemon Additions) | City | footprint 8 / 12 / 4 |

Ancient Cities are checked first; a swamp witch hut is deliberately not a village. Nether fortresses and bastions use `pieces`: their pieces tile their interiors and walkways, so their boxes already are the structure. No other structure is mapped (strongholds, trail ruins, pillager outposts and the rest fall through to the biome).

### Why village battles used to select Forest or Grassy

A generated village is a loose scatter of building, path and plaza pieces with open ground between them. Players and wild Pokémon stand on that ground. The first implementation asked `StructureAccessor.getStructureContaining(pos, structure)`, which only reports a structure when the position is inside one *piece's* bounding box (confirmed in the 1.21.1 bytecode: the method scans `StructurePiece.getBoundingBox().contains(pos)`), so most of a village (streets, plazas, gaps between houses) was classified as its biome. The existing live check passed because it stood at the exact centre of a piece.

### Containment policy

`containment` is per row (`StructureGeometry.Policy`):

- `pieces`: only blocks inside a piece box (compact structures).
- `footprint`: each piece box widened horizontally by `horizontal` blocks, extended `above` blocks over its top and `below` blocks under its floor. The margin is anchored to real pieces, not to the structure centre or its chunk, so a footprint can never become an unbounded radius, and the vertical limits keep caves under the village and open sky above it out. Villages use 8 horizontal (a street and a garden), 12 above (rooftops and tall builds) and 4 below (a cellar, not a cave).

### Detection

At battle pre-start on the server thread, for the anchor position (the wild Pokémon, else the first player) and then each player, in battle order:

1. the chunk's structure *references* list the structures that touch it (data already stored in the chunk);
2. each referenced structure is looked up in a per-server, per-registry, per-catalog-revision identity map of *mapped* structures, built once from the structure registry (IDs and tag membership resolved then);
3. for mapped structures only, the start chunk's `StructureStart` is read for its piece boxes and the row's policy decides containment.

Only chunks that are already loaded are read: a battle never loads or generates terrain, nothing scans the world, and no registry or tag work happens per battle. When several mapped structures contain a position, the row order above decides. The cache is invalidated by registry or catalog revision, and a datapack reload replaces the catalog atomically.

### Debug evidence

Every selection records bounded evidence in the battle's origin (`FieldApi.origin`), which is logged with the battle-start timings: the source, the field, the row, the structure ID, the participant that was inside (anchor or player, with its position), the piece box and the policy that matched, and a count of rejected candidates. At most a handful of candidates are listed.

### Tests

`StructureVerification` uses realistic API-shaped fixtures (chunk reference maps, structure starts with piece lists, registry and tag maps) rather than a synthetic hit: village streets and plazas inside the footprint, one block beyond each margin, below the cellar limit, above the rooftop limit, caves, overlapping structures, each mapped modded variant, participants other than the anchor, cache invalidation and not-loaded chunks. The isolated live check (`research/live_check.py --integration`) locates a real generated village, battles at a street gap outside every piece box, and records its distance.

## Biome mapping changes (2026-10-05)

| Biome | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `minecraft:plains` | Grassy Terrain | Grassy Terrain (unchanged) |
| `minecraft:sunflower_plains` | Flower Garden (matched the generator's `flower` pattern) | **Grassy Terrain** |
| `minecraft:mushroom_fields` | Corrosive Field | **Fairy Tale Field** (and Cave when ≥12 blocks under its surface with no sky, like other surface biomes) |
| future biome IDs containing `mushroom` | Corrosive Field | **Fairy Tale Field** |

`minecraft:snowy_plains` stays Icy Field (a snow biome). `terralith:cave/fungal_caves` stays Corrosive Field: it is a cave, not a mushroom-field equivalent, and the Terralith pack is disabled in this world. Terralith's alpha islands are already replaced by `minecraft:mushroom_fields` through this profile's biome replacer, so they also become Fairy Tale Field.

## Indexing and reload

`RejuvenationFields.reload` builds the catalog, validates it and compiles `EnvironmentResolver.Index` (underwater rows, structure rows, biome rows with per-biome candidate lists) once per datapack revision. A reload replaces the whole catalog atomically; the structure identity map is rebuilt lazily for the new revision and registry. Battle start therefore reads entity/world state, looks up the cached index and selects the field.
